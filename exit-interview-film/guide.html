Exit Interviews — how the process runs

A guide for the team  ·  October 2026

When somebody resigns, the exit interview is now scheduled, recorded, written up, filed and sent back on the original email thread without anyone having to start it. Two steps still wait for a person: choosing the time, and reading the record before it goes.

1From the resignation email to the filed record

Nobody has to start this.

  1. A resignation arrives. It does not have to say “resignation” in the subject line. One written in the body counts, and so does a forwarded one where a colleague adds a line on top asking for an exit interview to be arranged.
  2. You are emailed that a case is open and needs a time.
  3. You pick a slot. The calendar invite goes to the person leaving, to whoever asked for the interview, to People & Culture, and to the notetaker that records the call.
  4. The notetaker’s summary is collected once the interview has happened. No one forwards it on.
  5. The record is written in the same house style as the ones done by hand, rendered as a PDF, and you are emailed that a draft is waiting.
  6. You read it and send. The PDF is filed in the Exit Interviews folder in Drive and replied back onto the original email thread, with the record attached.

From the resignation landing to the record being filed, the only time it waits on a person is at steps 3 and 6.

2The two decisions a person still makes

These were left human deliberately, not because they were hard to automate.

Choosing the time

Somebody who has just resigned is not a calendar slot. The date is picked by hand, and the person leaving, the colleague who flagged it, People & Culture and the notetaker all go on the same invite.

Reading the record before it is sent

An exit record carries what one person said about named colleagues, and sometimes a harassment disclosure. It is read in full before it goes, and the wording can be corrected first — from a phone, without opening a laptop.

What can be changed: the reasons, the sections, who was named and how, and the person’s own quoted words. What cannot: the shape of the document — no section can be added, removed or reordered, and no name swapped for another. Once a record has been sent it is locked.

3Where the records are, and who can open them

Every finished record is a PDF in the Exit Interviews folder in Drive, and everyone on the reply is given read access as it is filed. Nobody has to ask for access.

The record also arrives as a reply on the original email thread, with the PDF attached, so it reaches the people who were already part of that conversation without anyone being copied in by hand afterwards.

4What protects the person who left

The record never goes to them. It names their manager and sometimes carries an allegation, so it is the one email that must not reach its own subject. Their address is removed from every reply, and if it is put back by hand the send is refused outright — before the record is filed anywhere. The check also catches a second address they may have used on the thread.
It never goes to a wide list either. A resignation is sometimes sent to a whole department. Replying to all of that would put a confidential record in front of the organisation, so addresses like all@ or everyone@ are left off and named on the card, to be added back only on purpose.
They are told the call is recorded. The invite says a notetaker joins and records, that the notes become a record for People & Culture, that the recording itself is not circulated, and that they can say at the start if they would rather it was not recorded.
They are told what it is not tied to. The invite says plainly that nothing said is tied to their clearance, their final settlement or their references, and that they do not have to answer anything.
The record carries their words, not ours. Where the notetaker preserved what they actually said, the sentence is kept exactly — grammar untidied, Urdu untranslated — and set apart in the document so a reader can see where our summary stops and their account begins. Where no words of theirs survive, the space is left empty rather than filled with a plausible quote.
Sections that travel badly are marked. A harassment disclosure or a named allegation is flagged inside the document and in the covering email as restricted, so the boundary survives the document being forwarded.

5What it does not do

It does not read the recording

It reads the notetaker’s written summary, which is already a paraphrase of the conversation. The record is therefore only as good as those notes — which is why Rumi is the notetaker on the invite: it keeps verbatim quotes, and Fathom’s recap does not.

It does not judge

The record reports what was said in neutral terms. It adds no severity rating, no overall assessment and no recommendations of its own, and it never leaves out what the person said worked. Those judgements belong to the people reading it.

It cannot reissue a corrected record

Once a record has been sent it cannot be amended through the app. A correction has to be handled by hand for now.

It says when it has broken

If it stops working — an expired login, a service down — Tahir is emailed, and again when it recovers. Silence means nothing arrived, not that something failed quietly.

If something looks wrong, say so. In particular: if a case appears for something that was never a resignation, if a record names someone incorrectly, or if you were expecting a record and none arrived. Nothing is sent without being read first, so a mistake caught at that point costs nothing.
